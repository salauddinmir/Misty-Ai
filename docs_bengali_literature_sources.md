# Bengali Literature Training Sources and Policy Notes

## Verified source categories

1. **Bengali Wikisource** provides a Bengali language and literature portal organized into history, grammar, poetry, drama, fiction (novels, short stories, folklore), religion, non-fiction, biographies and autobiographies. It lists works and transcription projects, including historical literary criticism and public-domain-era texts.
   Source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Portal:Bengali_language_and_literature

2. **Wikisource Bengali Literature** includes Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's 1871 English essay on Bengali literature and discusses historical schools including Vaishnavite lyric poetry, Bengali adaptations of the Ramayana and Mahabharata, Mangalkavya and later literary periods.
   Source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bengali_Literature

3. **Project Gutenberg** provides legally free eBooks whose US copyright has expired. Folk-Tales of Bengal by Lal Behari Day is identified as public domain in the USA and includes Bengali folklore themes and story metadata.
   Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/38488

4. **Project Gutenberg** also provides Tales of Bengal by S. B. Banerjea, identified as public domain in the USA, with story-level contents and Bengal social-life metadata.
   Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10999

## Training policy

MISTY will not ingest or reproduce complete copyrighted books unless the user supplies the text with authorization or the work is clearly public domain/open licensed. The initial literature package will use public-domain or openly licensed works, bibliographic metadata, literary taxonomy, author/work relationships, short factual summaries, themes, genres, motifs, historical periods, and user-provided authorized text. For copyrighted contemporary works, MISTY will provide high-level summaries, criticism, and bibliographic facts rather than lengthy location-based or verbatim reproduction.

## Planned taxonomy

- History and periods: Charyapada, medieval literature, Vaishnava পদাবলী, Mangalkavya, nineteenth-century Bengal Renaissance, modern and postcolonial Bengali literature.
- Genres: poetry, novel, novella, short story, drama, essay, biography, autobiography, folklore, children’s literature, science fiction, detective fiction, travel writing, literary criticism.
- Knowledge relations: author_of, belongs_to_genre, belongs_to_period, has_theme, has_character, adapted_from, translated_as, influenced_by, setting, language, and source_license.

## Important limitation

The source pages establish useful catalog and public-domain leads, not blanket permission for every digitized Bengali book. Each work must be checked individually for jurisdiction, author death date, edition rights, repository terms, and license before full-text ingestion.
